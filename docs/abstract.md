# Abstract

This thesis estimates the price elasticity of residential natural gas demand in
Ireland using quarterly data from 2014 Q1 to 2024 Q4 (N = 44) within an Autoregressive
Distributed Lag (ARDL) bounds-testing framework. Because gas and electricity prices are
highly collinear (r = 0.990), the model is reparameterised around an identified
energy-price term and a relative-price term. Demand is price-inelastic and
significant: the static elasticity is −0.334 (p = 0.006) and the ARDL long-run
elasticity is −0.367 (p = 0.012). A bounds test confirms cointegration (F = 48.487
against an upper bound of 3.79), with 87.9% of any disequilibrium corrected within one
quarter. Neither the 2020 pandemic nor the 2022 energy crisis produces a statistically
detectable structural break, given the sample's detection limits, though the near-zero
crisis effect is window-dependent. Two measurement problems are documented rather than
dismissed: heating degree days behave as a near-deterministic seasonal profile rather
than a genuine weather signal, and the available Building Energy Rating proxy tracks
property transactions rather than thermal performance. The results imply that carbon
taxation alone is unlikely to accelerate residential fuel switching in the short run,
and that price-based policy needs complementary capital measures to translate into
decarbonisation.
